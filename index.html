Scope

The fleet at a glance

Hand pumps only (live). Status is the last thing anyone recorded about each pump in 2026 — a call-centre contact, a preventive-maintenance visit or a repair.

Working includes pumps whose guardian described them to the call centre as "partially working". That label is not used as a status: in most cases the guardian is requesting the routine service visit (the pump delivers water and is due for scheduled maintenance), and the remainder are reduced-performance reports (mechanical, low yield, water quality) which are tracked separately below for management follow-up. Unknown = no call, visit or repair record in 2026 at all. Time since last visit counts from the most recent preventive visit or repair, or — where there has been neither — from the date the pump was rehabilitated or constructed, so a pump commissioned last month is not counted as overdue. Pumps with no works record of any kind sit in their own bucket and are excluded from the overdue count until they are commissioned in mWater.

Management notes — internal, remove before sharing with a VVB

1. Call-centre wording. Guardians who report "partially working" mostly write "désinfection" or "maintenance préventive" in the free-text field — they are asking for the scheduled service visit, and "disinfection" is the word they have been taught for it. Read literally by an outsider it implies the water is unsafe, which it is not (the pumps carry passed E. coli tests). Action: retrain guardians and the call team to log "service visit requested", and change the call form so that option exists; keep "disinfection" for the post-repair procedure only.

2. Field productivity. Median 3 pumps per field day is low for pumps a few km apart. One likely driver is the per-diem system, which pays per field day and so rewards slow coverage over completed pumps. Needs solving urgently — e.g. pay per pump serviced and verified in mWater, with the route plan as the reference — before the catch-up campaign starts.

Sampling methodology — evidence already on file
  • Sampling frame. The 908 water points managed by MadAvance in mWater. A point is eligible if it has a final SDWS 3 result passing the health-based rule (Escherichia coli 0 per 100 ml; arsenic ≤ 10 µg/L; fluoride ≤ 1.5 mg/L; nitrate ≤ 50 mg/L and manganese ≤ 0.08 mg/L where measured), is not abandoned, and is not in Beloha district (Marolinta, which sits outside the carbon programme). pH, electrical conductivity, turbidity and iron never disqualify a source. Current eligible counts: 126 in stratum HP-FD (Fort-Dauphin / Anosy) and 601 in HP-MA (Maroantsetra).
  • Design. Two-stage cluster sample: 12 sources drawn with probability proportional to size (PPS) across communes, plus 20 % replacements, then 5 households per source selected in the field by the k-th-household walking rule. Design effect (DEFF) = 1 + (m − 1) × intra-cluster correlation (ICC); at ICC 0.1 that is 1.4, so a nominal 60 samples gives an effective sample size of about 43.
  • The sampler. A purpose-built draw tool, sanitap-sampler, reads the frame live from mWater, applies the eligibility rule, performs the PPS draw and emits a signed sampling record. This is the instrument that makes the draw reproducible rather than discretionary.
  • Reproducibility. The random seed is the round name plus the stratum (for example 2026R1-HP-FD). The same seed against the same frame reproduces the identical draw, which has been verified on the deployed tool. A validation and verification body (VVB) can therefore re-run our draw rather than take it on trust.
  • Audit record. Each round is filed append-only under records/<round>/<stratum>/ as a sampling-record PDF with the machine-readable audit trail embedded as an attachment, alongside the selection in Excel and comma-separated values (CSV). No water point coordinates are ever published in these records.
  • Field reach rule. A drawn source more than 25 km from every other selected source and from the district town is flagged; it may be replaced in the field only if genuinely unreachable, and the reason is recorded on the sampling record.
  • Link to the laboratory record. The record identifier (which is the seed) is entered on every point-of-use and point-of-collection response in mWater, so each household result joins back to the draw that selected it.
  • Field protocol. The mWater point-of-use survey follows Annex B of water quality protocol v2.2: test type (point of use, point of collection, blank, duplicate, positive control), consent, container and spout condition, sample collection, filling and transport, processing, incubation reading, result and supervisor review. Pass thresholds: E. coli < 10 per 100 ml at point of use and < 1 per 100 ml at point of collection and for blanks.

Mapping rules: how the 1 km service area is drawn

What parameter SDWS 1 requires, what this report does about each requirement, and where we are not yet compliant. Stated here so it is on the record rather than discovered at verification.

The rule, as written. Gold Standard, Emission reductions from Safe Drinking Water Supply v1.0, parameter SDWS 1: “End users premises (e.g. households, institutions) within 1 km distance of project water source or a total collection time of 30 minutes or less for a round trip, including queuing, using the travel modes of walking or pedaling. … If there are any insurmountable barriers (e.g. river without bridge) transecting the circle defined by the 1 km radius or 30 mins distance, then end-users on the far side of the barrier shall be excluded from the count.”

RequirementWhat this report doesStatus
1 km service area, demonstrated by satellite imaging or GPS coordinatesEvery water point carries a GPS fix in the mWater register. The service area is a 1 km buffer computed on a local equidistant projection, so the radius is a true 1,000 m on the ground rather than a degree approximation.applied
No double counting between neighbouring water pointsWhere service areas overlap, the population in the overlap is divided equally between every water point that reaches it. A cell reachable from three pumps contributes one third to each. Nobody is counted twice.applied
Insurmountable barriers — end users on the far side shall be excludedApplied. Coastline, rivers and canals form the barrier network, minus any reach carrying a bridge or a ford, since the methodology requires the barrier to be insurmountable. Only the polygon fragment containing the pump is kept. 627 of 736 points have at least one barrier transecting their circle; 122 lose more than a tenth of their service area.applied
Capacity ceiling as a conservativeness safeguardReported population is min(allocated, pump capacity): Canzee 500, India Mark 300.applied
Survey households must lie inside the 1 km radiusThe sampler draws only from registered water points and the field rule selects households walking out from the source. The barrier constraint must be added to the sampler when the clip is built.partial

Why the overlap rule matters more here than the barrier rule. In Maroantsetra the water points sit very close together: every one of the 604 has at least one neighbour whose service area overlaps its own, the average point overlaps 28 others, the most crowded overlaps 59, and 1,169 pairs of pumps in the portfolio stand within 200 m of each other. After the barrier clip and the equal split, the average Maroantsetra point is allocated 144 people and only 3 of 604 reach the capacity ceiling — so in Maroantsetra the figure is set by the overlap rule, not by the cap. Fort-Dauphin is the opposite: an average allocation of 615 with 78 of 127 points capped. This is also why the two sites diverge against the roof count in opposite directions.

Equal splitting is a choice, and it is the choice that reports the highest total. The methodology requires only that nobody is counted twice; it does not prescribe how to divide a shared population. Dividing equally ignores the fact that a household usually walks to its nearest pump, so a distance-weighted or nearest-source assignment would be more realistic. It would not, however, raise the reported total. The population inside the service areas is fixed, so any rule that divides it differently moves people between pumps without changing the sum — it can only change the total through the capacity ceiling, and concentrating people onto fewer points pushes more of them above that ceiling, where the excess is discarded. An earlier edition of this page stated the opposite; that was wrong. Equal splitting is retained for now because it is simple to state and simple to reproduce, and the nearest-source and inverse-distance-weighted variants have now been run alongside it. On the raster of record, without barriers, equal reports 91,957, inverse-distance-weighted 91,067 and nearest-source 88,454 — confirming that a more realistic allocation lowers the reported total rather than raising it. The comparison is filed in the evidence library.

Where the allocation rule does matter. It sets the people-per-pump figure, which drives maintenance cost per credit — 155 people per pump in Maroantsetra against 338 in Fort-Dauphin. And it governs how a monitored-volume sample must be stratified: a pump in a ten-pump village and a standalone pump are not interchangeable.

Who is counted as served: the annual usage survey

The 1 km service area answers parameter SDWS 1 — who lives near enough. It does not answer parameter SDWS 26 — who actually draws water from our pump. These are different numbers and the methodology asks for both.

The rule, as written. Gold Standard, Emission reductions from Safe Drinking Water Supply v1.0, parameter SDWS 26, HHp,y — number of premises served: a survey of the premises within 1 km of the project water source, checking how often each used the project water source during the year. Only premises reporting use at least every two days may be counted. Annual.

It is a sample, not a census. Surveying every household around 743 hand pumps is neither required nor possible. The registered validated project activity design document for this programme sets the method: the project database drives a digital randomisation tool that randomly selects the water points to be visited for the annual monitoring survey; end users at those points are then selected by the random-walk method, by random selection of premises from rooftop satellite imagery, or by another representative and unbiased method. The survey must reach a 90% confidence interval with a 10% margin of error, minimum 100 households. Parameter SDWS 26 is reported as a mean (premises served per water point) together with the proportion reporting use at least every two days.

What this means for the figures on this page. The people-served number here is a modelled residential population inside the service areas. It is the SDWS 1 boundary count, and it is the right basis for planning and for this report. It is not the SDWS 26 figure, and the SDWS 26 figure will be lower, because it counts only premises that report using the pump at least every other day. The two are reconciled by the usage survey, not by the model.
